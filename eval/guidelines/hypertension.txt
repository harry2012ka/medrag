# SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL TEXT written for the MedRAG accuracy benchmark. It is not an official clinical guideline, contains no patient data, and must not be used for patient care.
=== page 1 ===
Adult Hypertension: Definition and Measurement

Hypertension is diagnosed when the average office blood pressure is 130/80 mmHg or higher
on two or more readings taken at two or more separate visits. Stage 1 hypertension is a
systolic pressure of 130 to 139 mmHg or a diastolic pressure of 80 to 89 mmHg. Stage 2
hypertension is a systolic pressure of 140 mmHg or higher or a diastolic pressure of
90 mmHg or higher.

Blood pressure should be measured with the patient seated quietly for five minutes, back
supported, feet flat on the floor, and the arm supported at heart level. Use a cuff whose
bladder encircles at least 80 percent of the upper arm. Home blood pressure monitoring or
24-hour ambulatory monitoring is recommended to confirm the diagnosis and to detect white
coat hypertension or masked hypertension.
=== page 2 ===
Lifestyle Therapy

All adults with elevated blood pressure or hypertension should receive lifestyle advice.
Effective measures include reducing dietary sodium to less than 1,500 mg per day, following
a DASH-style eating pattern rich in fruit, vegetables and low-fat dairy, losing weight if
overweight, and doing at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week.
Alcohol should be limited to no more than two drinks per day for men and one drink per day
for women. Potassium-rich foods are encouraged unless the patient has advanced kidney
disease.
=== page 3 ===
Drug Therapy

Medication is started for stage 1 hypertension when the estimated 10-year cardiovascular
risk is 10 percent or higher, and for all patients with stage 2 hypertension. First-line
drug classes are thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics, long-acting dihydropyridine calcium
channel blockers, ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers. For stage 2
hypertension, starting two first-line agents from different classes is reasonable.

An ACE inhibitor and an angiotensin receptor blocker should not be combined. Check serum
potassium and creatinine within two to four weeks after starting or increasing an ACE
inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker. The usual treatment target for most adults is
below 130/80 mmHg.
=== page 4 ===
Hypertensive Urgency and Resistant Hypertension

Resistant hypertension is blood pressure above target despite three drugs of different
classes at optimal doses, one of which is a diuretic. Before adding a fourth drug, check
adherence, confirm readings out of the office, and look for secondary causes such as
primary aldosteronism, obstructive sleep apnea and renal artery stenosis. Spironolactone
is the preferred fourth agent.

A blood pressure of 180/120 mmHg or higher with signs of acute organ damage, such as chest
pain, neurological deficit or acute kidney injury, is a hypertensive emergency and needs
immediate hospital treatment.
