# SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL TEXT written for the MedRAG accuracy benchmark. It is not an official clinical guideline, contains no patient data, and must not be used for patient care.
=== page 1 ===
Chronic Kidney Disease: Definition and Staging

Chronic kidney disease is an abnormality of kidney structure or function present for more
than three months. It is defined by an estimated glomerular filtration rate below
60 mL/min/1.73 m2, or by markers of kidney damage such as albuminuria, for at least three
months. Staging uses both the filtration rate category, G1 to G5, and the albuminuria
category, A1 to A3.

Stage G3a is a filtration rate of 45 to 59, G3b is 30 to 44, G4 is 15 to 29, and G5,
kidney failure, is below 15. Albuminuria category A2 is a urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio
of 30 to 300 mg/g, and A3 is above 300 mg/g.
=== page 2 ===
Slowing Progression

Treat patients who have chronic kidney disease and albuminuria with an ACE inhibitor or an
angiotensin receptor blocker at the highest tolerated dose. An SGLT2 inhibitor is
recommended for most patients with an eGFR of 20 or more, with or without diabetes, to slow
loss of kidney function. Aim for a blood pressure below 130/80 mmHg.

Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, which can reduce kidney function and raise
potassium. Review all medicines and adjust doses to the current filtration rate.
=== page 3 ===
Complications and Monitoring

Monitor for anemia, metabolic acidosis, high potassium and mineral and bone disorder as
kidney function declines. Check hemoglobin at least once a year in stage G3 and more often
in later stages. For a serum potassium above 5.5 mmol/L, review diet and drugs that raise
potassium.

Oral sodium bicarbonate may be considered when serum bicarbonate stays below 18 mmol/L.
Iodinated contrast for imaging carries a risk of contrast-associated kidney injury;
ensure the patient is well hydrated beforehand.
=== page 4 ===
Referral to Nephrology

Refer to a kidney specialist when the eGFR is below 30, when the urine
albumin-to-creatinine ratio is persistently above 300 mg/g, when kidney function falls
rapidly, or when hypertension stays uncontrolled despite four or more drugs. Patients
approaching kidney failure should receive education about dialysis, kidney transplant and
conservative care well before treatment is needed.
