# SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL TEXT written for the MedRAG accuracy benchmark. It is not an official clinical guideline, contains no patient data, and must not be used for patient care.
=== page 1 ===
Fever in Children: Definition and Measurement

Fever in a child is a temperature of 38.0 C (100.4 F) or higher. In infants under 3 months,
a rectal reading is the most reliable. From 3 months to about 4 years, an electronic
axillary or tympanic reading is acceptable. Forehead strip thermometers are unreliable and
should not be used.

How high the temperature is does not by itself tell you how ill the child is, but any
fever in a baby under 3 months should be assessed by a clinician the same day. Parents'
reports that their child feels hot should be taken seriously even if no thermometer was
used.
=== page 2 ===
Red Flags and Young Infants

Every infant 28 days old or younger with a fever needs a full sepsis evaluation, including
blood, urine and cerebrospinal fluid cultures, admission to hospital and intravenous
antibiotics while the results are awaited. Infants aged 29 to 60 days can be risk
stratified with a urinalysis and inflammatory markers such as procalcitonin and C-reactive
protein.

At any age, warning signs of serious illness include pale, mottled or blue skin, a rash
that does not fade under pressure, a bulging fontanelle, neck stiffness, grunting or fast
breathing, a child who cannot be woken or does not respond normally, and fewer wet
diapers or reduced fluid intake. A child with any of these signs needs urgent face-to-face
assessment.
=== page 3 ===
Medicines for a Feverish Child

Medicines are given to make a distressed child more comfortable, not to bring the number
down. Acetaminophen (paracetamol) can be used from birth at 15 mg/kg every 4 to 6 hours,
with no more than 4 doses in 24 hours. Ibuprofen can be used in children older than 3
months who weigh more than 5 kg, at 10 mg/kg every 6 to 8 hours, and should be avoided in a
child who is dehydrated.

Do not routinely alternate or combine the two medicines; switch only if the child stays
distressed before the next dose is due. Aspirin must not be given to anyone under 16
because of the risk of Reye syndrome. Fever medicines do not prevent febrile seizures.
Tepid sponging and cold baths are not recommended.
=== page 4 ===
Febrile Seizures and Home Care

A simple febrile seizure happens between 6 months and 5 years of age, is generalized,
lasts less than 15 minutes and does not recur within 24 hours. It does not need routine
EEG, brain imaging or blood tests if the child recovers fully. A seizure that is focal,
lasts longer than 15 minutes or repeats within 24 hours is complex and needs further
assessment.

At home, offer regular fluids, dress the child in light clothing and check on them during
the night. Keep the child away from nursery or school while the fever lasts. Seek review
if the fever lasts more than 5 days, which should raise the possibility of Kawasaki
disease, or if there are signs of dehydration, a new rash, or the parent is worried.
