# SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL TEXT written for the MedRAG accuracy benchmark. It is not an official clinical guideline, contains no patient data, and must not be used for patient care.
=== page 1 ===
Prenatal Care: The First Visit

Confirm the pregnancy and set the due date with a first-trimester ultrasound measurement
of crown-rump length. Everyone who could become pregnant should take folic acid 0.4 mg
(400 micrograms) daily from before conception; a 4 mg daily dose is used after a previous
pregnancy affected by a neural tube defect.

First-visit tests include blood group and Rh type, an antibody screen, a complete blood
count, rubella immunity, HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis and a urine culture for bacteria
without symptoms. Review all current medicines and stop any that are unsafe in pregnancy.
=== page 2 ===
Screening Through Pregnancy

Offer screening for chromosomal conditions to everyone; cell-free DNA testing can be done
from 10 weeks. A detailed anatomy ultrasound is done at 18 to 22 weeks. Screen for
gestational diabetes at 24 to 28 weeks, usually with a 1-hour 50 gram glucose challenge.
Take a vaginal-rectal swab for group B streptococcus at 36 to 37 weeks.

If the mother is Rh-negative and not sensitized, give anti-D immunoglobulin at about 28
weeks and again within 72 hours after the birth of an Rh-positive baby, and after any
bleeding or abdominal trauma.
=== page 3 ===
Vaccines, Weight and Lifestyle

Influenza vaccine can be given in any trimester. Give Tdap between 27 and 36 weeks in every
pregnancy to protect the newborn from whooping cough. RSV vaccine is offered at 32 to 36
weeks during RSV season. Live vaccines such as MMR and varicella are avoided until after
delivery.

Recommended total weight gain depends on pre-pregnancy BMI: 11.5 to 16 kg for a normal BMI
and 5 to 9 kg for BMI 30 or more. No amount of alcohol is known to be safe. Keep caffeine
under 200 mg a day, avoid high-mercury fish such as swordfish, and avoid unpasteurized
milk and soft cheeses.
=== page 4 ===
Visit Schedule and Warning Signs

Routine visits are every 4 weeks until 28 weeks, every 2 weeks until 36 weeks and weekly
after that. Preeclampsia is blood pressure of 140/90 or higher after 20 weeks with protein
in the urine or other severe features. People at high risk of preeclampsia should take
low-dose aspirin 81 mg daily starting between 12 and 28 weeks, ideally before 16 weeks.

Seek care straight away for vaginal bleeding, leaking fluid, fewer baby movements, a
severe headache or vision changes, swelling of the face or hands, or regular contractions
before 37 weeks.
