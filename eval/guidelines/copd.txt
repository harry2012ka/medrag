=== page 1 ===
COPD: Diagnosis and Severity

Suspect chronic obstructive pulmonary disease in adults over 35 with a smoking or
occupational exposure history who report breathlessness on exertion, chronic cough,
regular sputum or frequent winter chest infections. The diagnosis is confirmed by
spirometry showing a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio below 0.70.

Airflow limitation is graded by FEV1 as a percentage of predicted: grade 1 is 80 percent or
more, grade 2 is 50 to 79 percent, grade 3 is 30 to 49 percent and grade 4 is under 30
percent. Symptoms are scored with the mMRC breathlessness scale or the CAT questionnaire.
Every person with COPD should be tested once for alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.
=== page 2 ===
Stable COPD Treatment

Stopping smoking is the single most effective step at any stage. Start with a long-acting
bronchodilator; a long-acting muscarinic antagonist combined with a long-acting
beta-agonist is preferred when symptoms are marked. Add an inhaled corticosteroid only
for people who still have exacerbations and a blood eosinophil count of 300 cells per
microliter or more. Avoid inhaled corticosteroids when eosinophils are below 100, because
they raise the risk of pneumonia.

Offer pulmonary rehabilitation to people limited by breathlessness and within 4 weeks
after a hospital admission. Recommended vaccines are yearly influenza, pneumococcal,
COVID-19, RSV for older adults and Tdap for those not vaccinated in adolescence.
=== page 3 ===
COPD Exacerbations

An exacerbation is a worsening of breathlessness, cough or sputum over less than 14 days.
Treat with short-acting bronchodilators and oral prednisone 40 mg daily for 5 days.
Give an antibiotic when sputum turns purulent together with more breathlessness or more
sputum, or when ventilatory support is needed; amoxicillin-clavulanate, doxycycline or a
macrolide for 5 days are usual choices.

Aim for an oxygen saturation of 88 to 92 percent, because high-flow oxygen can worsen
carbon dioxide retention. Start noninvasive ventilation for acute hypercapnic respiratory
failure with an arterial pH below 7.35. Review inhaler technique before discharge.
=== page 4 ===
Oxygen and Advanced COPD

Long-term oxygen therapy improves survival when resting PaO2 is 55 mmHg or lower or
saturation is 88 percent or lower, confirmed twice over 3 weeks while stable. It should be
used for at least 15 hours a day. It is also offered at PaO2 56 to 59 mmHg with right
heart failure or polycythemia. Smoking near oxygen is a fire risk.

For frequent exacerbations despite triple inhaled therapy, consider roflumilast if there
is chronic bronchitis and FEV1 is under 50 percent, or long-term azithromycin in former
smokers after checking the QT interval and hearing. Low-dose oral opioids can ease
refractory breathlessness in advanced disease, alongside advance care planning.
