=== page 1 ===
Asthma in Adults: Diagnosis

Asthma causes variable respiratory symptoms such as wheeze, shortness of breath, chest
tightness and cough, together with variable expiratory airflow limitation. Spirometry is
the preferred test. An increase in FEV1 of more than 12 percent and more than 200 mL after
a bronchodilator supports the diagnosis. Peak flow variability or a methacholine challenge
test can help when spirometry is normal.
=== page 2 ===
Stepwise Inhaler Treatment

Every adult with asthma should have an inhaled corticosteroid. Short-acting beta-agonist
inhalers used alone, without an inhaled corticosteroid, are no longer recommended. The
preferred reliever is a combination inhaler of budesonide and formoterol taken as needed.
For persistent symptoms, use budesonide-formoterol as both daily maintenance and reliever
therapy.

If asthma remains uncontrolled on medium-dose inhaled corticosteroid with a long-acting
beta-agonist, check inhaler technique and adherence, then consider adding a long-acting
muscarinic antagonist such as tiotropium and refer for specialist assessment.
=== page 3 ===
Exacerbations and Action Plans

Give every patient a written asthma action plan. A moderate exacerbation should be treated
with repeated bronchodilator doses and a short course of oral prednisolone, typically 40 to
50 mg daily for five to seven days. Signs of a life-threatening attack include a peak flow
below 33 percent of best, oxygen saturation below 92 percent, a silent chest, cyanosis and
exhaustion; such patients need urgent hospital care.

Review the patient within two working days after an exacerbation. Annual influenza
vaccination is recommended.
