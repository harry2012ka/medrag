# SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL TEXT written for the MedRAG accuracy benchmark. It is not an official clinical guideline, contains no patient data, and must not be used for patient care.
=== page 1 ===
Anticoagulation Basics: Who Needs It

In atrial fibrillation not caused by a mechanical valve or moderate to severe mitral
stenosis, stroke risk is estimated with the CHA2DS2-VASc score. Oral anticoagulation is
recommended for men with a score of 2 or more and women with a score of 3 or more, and
should be considered for men scoring 1 and women scoring 2. Aspirin alone is not an
acceptable substitute for stroke prevention.

Bleeding risk can be estimated with HAS-BLED. A score of 3 or more marks higher bleeding
risk. It should prompt correction of modifiable factors such as uncontrolled blood
pressure, alcohol excess and unnecessary antiplatelet drugs, and closer follow-up, but on
its own it is not a reason to withhold anticoagulation.

A provoked deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism is treated for at least 3 months.
Unprovoked or recurrent clots may need extended treatment after a review of bleeding risk.
=== page 2 ===
Direct Oral Anticoagulants

For nonvalvular atrial fibrillation, a direct oral anticoagulant is preferred over
warfarin because it causes less intracranial bleeding and needs no routine INR testing.
Direct oral anticoagulants should not be used with a mechanical heart valve or with
moderate to severe mitral stenosis.

Apixaban is given as 5 mg twice daily. The dose is lowered to 2.5 mg twice daily when the
patient has at least two of the following: age 80 or older, body weight 60 kg or less, or
serum creatinine 1.5 mg/dL or higher. Rivaroxaban is given as 20 mg once daily with the
evening meal, or 15 mg if creatinine clearance is 15 to 50 mL/min. Dabigatran is given as
150 mg twice daily and is largely cleared by the kidneys.

Kidney function should be checked at least once a year, and more often in older adults or
when kidney function is falling.
=== page 3 ===
Warfarin

The target INR is 2.0 to 3.0 for atrial fibrillation and venous thromboembolism, and 2.5
to 3.5 for a mechanical mitral valve. A usual starting dose is 5 mg daily; 2 to 3 mg is
more suitable for frail or older patients and those with liver disease or heart failure.

Check the INR every few days while starting treatment. Once the dose is stable, the
interval can be stretched to every 4 weeks, and up to 12 weeks for very stable patients.

Many drugs change the warfarin effect, including several antibiotics, amiodarone and
fluconazole, so check the INR within a week of starting or stopping an interacting drug.
Patients do not need to avoid vitamin K rich foods such as leafy greens, but should keep
their intake consistent from week to week.
=== page 4 ===
Bleeding, Reversal and Procedures

If the INR is above 9 without bleeding, hold warfarin and give oral vitamin K 2.5 to 5 mg.
For an INR between 4.5 and 9 without bleeding, hold one or two doses and recheck. For major
bleeding on warfarin, give intravenous vitamin K 10 mg together with four-factor
prothrombin complex concentrate.

Idarucizumab reverses dabigatran. Andexanet alfa can reverse apixaban and rivaroxaban;
prothrombin complex concentrate is an alternative where it is not available.

Before elective surgery, stop a direct oral anticoagulant 24 to 48 hours ahead depending
on bleeding risk, and longer when kidney function is reduced. Most patients with atrial
fibrillation who stop warfarin for surgery do not need heparin bridging.
