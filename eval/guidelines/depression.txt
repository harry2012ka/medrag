# SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL TEXT written for the MedRAG accuracy benchmark. It is not an official clinical guideline, contains no patient data, and must not be used for patient care.
=== page 1 ===
Depression Screening in Primary Care

Screen all adults for depression, including during pregnancy and after birth, when there
are systems in place to make sure a positive screen is followed up. A two-question
screen (PHQ-2) asks about low mood and loss of interest over the past two weeks; a positive
answer is followed by the nine-item PHQ-9.

PHQ-9 totals of 5, 10, 15 and 20 mark mild, moderate, moderately severe and severe
symptoms. A score of 10 or more usually indicates clinically important depression. Always
read the answer to item 9, which asks about thoughts of being better off dead or of
self-harm, whatever the total score.
=== page 2 ===
Diagnosis and Suicide Risk

Major depressive disorder needs at least five symptoms for at least two weeks, one of which
is depressed mood or loss of interest or pleasure. Before diagnosing, ask about past
episodes of mania or hypomania to rule out bipolar disorder, and consider hypothyroidism,
anemia, medicines and alcohol or drug use.

Ask about suicidal thoughts directly; asking does not put the idea in a person's head. A
person with a plan, intent or access to means needs same-day assessment and a safety
plan, including removal of firearms and stockpiled medicines. In the United States the 988
Suicide and Crisis Lifeline is available by call or text at any hour.
=== page 3 ===
Treatment

For mild depression, offer psychotherapy such as cognitive behavioral therapy or active
monitoring. For moderate to severe depression, offer psychotherapy, an antidepressant or
both together. A selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor such as sertraline or escitalopram
is the usual first medicine because it is well tolerated and safe in overdose.

Review within 2 weeks of starting, then judge the response after 4 to 6 weeks at a full
dose. If there is little improvement, raise the dose or switch to another medicine.
Continue treatment for 6 to 12 months after recovery, and longer after repeated episodes.
People under 25 can have more suicidal thoughts in the first weeks of an antidepressant
and need closer follow-up.
=== page 4 ===
Stopping Treatment and Special Groups

Antidepressants should be tapered over at least 4 weeks rather than stopped suddenly, to
avoid discontinuation symptoms such as dizziness, irritability, sleep disturbance and
flu-like feelings. In older adults, start low and go slow, for example sertraline 25 mg.

Sertraline is often chosen during pregnancy and breastfeeding after discussing risks and
benefits. Screen new mothers for postpartum depression at well-baby visits. Refer to
psychiatry for psychotic features, suspected bipolar disorder, or no response to two
adequate medicine trials.
