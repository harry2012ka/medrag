=== page 1 ===
Type 2 Diabetes: Screening and Diagnosis

Screen adults aged 35 to 70 who are overweight or obese, and adults of any age with
additional risk factors such as a family history of diabetes or a history of gestational
diabetes. Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed by a fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher,
a hemoglobin A1c of 6.5 percent or higher, a two-hour glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher on an
oral glucose tolerance test, or a random glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher with classic
symptoms. Without symptoms, an abnormal result should be repeated to confirm.

Prediabetes is a hemoglobin A1c of 5.7 to 6.4 percent or a fasting glucose of 100 to
125 mg/dL. People with prediabetes should be offered a structured lifestyle program aiming
for about 7 percent weight loss.
=== page 2 ===
Glycemic Targets and Monitoring

For most nonpregnant adults the hemoglobin A1c goal is below 7 percent. A less strict goal,
such as below 8 percent, may suit older adults with limited life expectancy or a history of
severe hypoglycemia. Measure hemoglobin A1c every three months until the goal is reached,
then at least twice a year.

Hypoglycemia is a blood glucose below 70 mg/dL. Treat it with 15 grams of fast-acting
carbohydrate, recheck after 15 minutes, and repeat if the glucose is still low. Sulfonylureas
and insulin carry the highest risk of hypoglycemia.
=== page 3 ===
Medication Choices

Metformin is the usual first medication, together with lifestyle change. Metformin should
not be started when the eGFR is below 45 and should be stopped when the eGFR falls below 30
mL/min/1.73 m2. Gastrointestinal upset is its most common side effect and can be reduced by
using the extended-release form and taking it with meals. Long-term use can lower vitamin
B12 levels.

For patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure or chronic kidney
disease, add an SGLT2 inhibitor or a GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven benefit, regardless
of the A1c. GLP-1 receptor agonists also produce meaningful weight loss. SGLT2 inhibitors can
cause genital yeast infections and, rarely, euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis.
=== page 4 ===
Complication Screening

Arrange a dilated eye examination at diagnosis and then every one to two years. Check urine
albumin-to-creatinine ratio and eGFR at least once a year. Examine the feet at every visit
and perform a monofilament test for loss of protective sensation at least once a year.
Most adults with diabetes aged 40 to 75 should take a moderate-intensity statin.
